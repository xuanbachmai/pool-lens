5 · Arbitrage, and who pays for it

The pool has no idea what anything is worth. It only learns when somebody trades it back into line — and that somebody is paid out of the liquidity providers' pockets. Inside the fee band no trade is worth making, which is why a higher fee tier bleeds less.

Where this connects

Each section above is a term in the real analysis:

  • Depth becomes the entry and exit cost of an actual position — on a thin pool the round trip can cost more than months of fees.
  • Impermanent loss is the thing fee income has to beat; the analyser measures both against the pool's real price history rather than a slider.
  • The fee band is the structural bleed: every move larger than the band hands an arbitrageur a rebalance at a stale price.
